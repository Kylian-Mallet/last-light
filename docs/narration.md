# Last light — narration

## The story

An old polar explorer remembers, decades later, the last evening of the austral summer,
when he was camped near an emperor penguin colony. He followed a chick there, which he
called **Mumble** for the small sounds it made "to no one in particular". That evening the
adults left for the sea; that night, a storm; at dawn, the chick was still there.

The narrator never comments on controls or objectives: he remembers. He speaks little,
lets the music and the wind breathe, and falls silent at the strongest moments (the
unanswered call, the heart of the storm). He never says "I" in a heroic tone: he was a
witness, nothing more. The last line reveals that he still looks for that chick every time
he goes back onto the ice.

**Voice:** English, warm, elderly, measured, a British or neutral explorer's accent, with
no trailer-style emphasis. Subtitles are in English or French, following the game's
language (on by default, can be turned off in Options › Display).

## What this means for the game

- **Opening (28 s):** lines 1 to 3 are about 22 s of speech. That fits, provided shot 3
  is about 2 s longer so that line 3 (≈ 11 s) does not run into the crane shot.
- **Departure:** lines 7 and 8 fit in shots 1 and 2; the music is already silent from
  20 to 30 s for the call. Line 9 is spoken at the end of the cinematic, when the player
  takes control again.
- **Storm:** a single line on the first shot; the other two shots have no voice, to let
  the wind and the music rise into the white.
- **Ending:** lines 18 and 19 share the crane shot and the title card. The card appears
  after line 18 instead of after a fixed delay.
- **Mix:** the music and the ambience duck by about 6 dB while the narrator speaks, then
  come back up.
- **Pacing in play:** the in-play lines (4, 10 to 14, 17) trigger only once, never over
  another line, and wait for an important call to finish.
- **The name:** "Mumble" is also the name of the hero of the film *Happy Feet*. For a
  personal demo this is fine; if the game were ever released publicly, another name
  would be safer.
